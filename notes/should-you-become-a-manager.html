Should you become a manager?

JP Park · 4 min read

Draft for JP: this is a starting point written from general views, not your stories. Rewrite in your own voice and add a real example from your career where marked.

Somewhere around year four or five, someone will ask if you want to manage. It usually sounds like a compliment, and it usually feels like the next step. Often it's neither.

Management isn't a promotion. It's a different job. Before you say yes, ask yourself these four things.

1. Do I like the work, or do I like the status?

Be honest. If your best days are the ones where you ship something, management will take that away from you. If your best days are the ones where someone you helped has a breakthrough, it might be for you.

2. Can I handle being the bad news?

You'll deliver feedback people don't want, reorgs you didn't choose, and decisions you disagree with. You're the buffer. That is the job.

The best managers I know didn't want power. They wanted their team to win.

3. Is there another way up?

In many companies there's a senior IC path with the same pay and more scope. Ask. If the answer is no, that tells you something about the company.

4. Can I try it before I commit?

Lead a project. Mentor someone. Run the team for a quarter. If your company lets you test the job, take it. Moving back is much easier if you haven't burned the bridge.
