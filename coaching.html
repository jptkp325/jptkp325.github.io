Two ways to work together

Career coaching and interview coaching.

Career coaching

For the bigger questions: a promotion case, a move into management, a rough patch with your manager, a decision about what's next. We work on it over regular sessions.

Interview coaching

For when you're interviewing for PM roles. Product sense, execution, strategy, and behavioral questions, with mock interviews and straight feedback from someone who has hired PMs. We can also work through offers and negotiation.

How it works

Simple on purpose.

  1. Free intro chat

    Thirty minutes. You tell me what's going on. I tell you honestly whether I can help, and if I can't, who might.

  2. We pick a focus

    A promotion case, an interview loop, a rough patch with your manager. We name the one thing worth working on first.

  3. We work on it

    Regular sessions, real homework, and straight feedback. For interviews that means mock interviews and a debrief. You leave each one with something to try.

Is this for you?

Good fit, and not.

We'll get along if…

  • You're a PM, 2–12 years into product, and at a decision point or in an interview process
  • You want honest feedback more than reassurance
  • You'll do the work between sessions
  • You're stuck on people stuff: managers, peers, politics

Probably not for…

  • Memorized answers or a script to recite. I'll help you think, not perform
  • Anyone looking for a guaranteed outcome
  • Executive-level coaching (I'd point you to someone better)

Questions

Stuff people ask first.

I'm not a PM. Can you still help?

Possibly. My focus is product managers, but I've spent my career working alongside engineers, designers, and data folks. If your question is about product-adjacent roles, scope, or a tricky manager, ask me in the intro chat and I'll tell you honestly whether I'm the right person.

Do you run mock interviews?

Yes. We practice the question types you'll actually face, then debrief honestly on what landed and what didn't. I've been on the hiring side, so I can tell you how an answer reads from the other chair.

What if I'm not sure what I want?

That's a perfectly good place to start. Most people come in with a vague itch, not a plan. Figuring out what's behind it is the first piece of work.

How is this different from a mentor?

A coach is on the hook for your progress and keeps you accountable. I'll also share what I've seen, but I'm not here to tell you to copy my path.

What happens in the intro chat?

We talk about your situation. I'll ask a lot of questions. At the end I'll tell you what I'd focus on and whether working together makes sense. There's no pressure to sign up.

Start with a conversation.

Tell me where you are. I'll tell you what I think. If it's a fit, great. If not, you still get a free thirty minutes with someone who's seen a lot.
