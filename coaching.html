Coaching

A thinking partner who's been in your seat.

One-on-one coaching for early and mid-career people in tech: product, engineering, design, data, and everyone in between.

How it works

Simple on purpose.

  1. Free intro chat

    Thirty minutes. You tell me what's going on. I tell you honestly whether I can help, and if I can't, who might.

  2. We pick a focus

    A promotion case, a job search, a rough patch with your manager. We name the one thing worth working on first.

  3. We work on it

    Regular sessions, real homework, and straight feedback. You leave each one with something to try.

Is this for you?

Good fit, and not.

We'll get along if…

  • You're 2–12 years into a tech career and at a decision point
  • You want honest feedback more than reassurance
  • You'll do the work between sessions
  • You're stuck on people stuff: managers, peers, politics

Probably not for…

  • Resume rewrites or interview-question drills (there are good services for that)
  • Anyone looking for a guaranteed outcome
  • Executive-level coaching (I'd point you to someone better)

Questions

Stuff people ask first.

Do I have to be a PM?

No. I've spent my career working alongside engineers, designers, and data folks. Career problems like unclear scope, a tricky manager, or a stalled promotion look much the same across roles. I'm most useful on product-adjacent questions.

What if I'm not sure what I want?

That's a perfectly good place to start. Most people come in with a vague itch, not a plan. Figuring out what's behind it is the first piece of work.

How is this different from a mentor?

A coach is on the hook for your progress and keeps you accountable. I'll also share what I've seen, but I'm not here to tell you to copy my path.

What happens in the intro chat?

We talk about your situation. I'll ask a lot of questions. At the end I'll tell you what I'd focus on and whether working together makes sense. There's no pressure to sign up.

Start with a conversation.

Tell me where you are. I'll tell you what I think. If it's a fit, great. If not, you still get a free thirty minutes with someone who's seen a lot.
