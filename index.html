The questions people bring me.

  • “I'm good at my job and I still feel stuck.”

    Usually it's not a skills problem. It's a visibility, scope, or “wrong seat” problem, and each has a different fix.

  • “Should I manage PMs, or stay hands-on?”

    Maybe. But not because it's the only way up. Let's figure out if you'd actually like the job.

  • “My manager and I just don't click.”

    Managing up is a learnable skill. So is knowing when to stop trying and move on.

  • “I keep reaching the final round and not getting the offer.”

    Usually it's one or two patterns, not a lack of ability. Mock interviews and honest feedback from someone who has hired PMs will find them.

  • “Product sense and execution questions make me freeze.”

    It's a skill, and it's learnable. We practice until your thinking comes out as clearly as it does on the job.

  • “Do I take the offer?”

    Startup or big company, more money or more growth. I'll help you think it through, not just hand you a pros and cons list.

What I believe

A few opinions, up front.

  1. Your career is not a ladder.

    Titles are a lagging indicator. Chase scope, learning, and people worth working for, and the titles tend to follow.

  2. Most career advice is written for someone else.

    What works for a group PM at a big tech company will not work for the first PM at a 40-person startup. Context beats playbooks.

  3. The hard conversations are the job.

    Asking for the raise, pushing back on a bad plan, telling your boss you're unhappy. The people who get good at these move faster than the ones who get good at slides.
